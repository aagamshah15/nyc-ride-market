## Question

Did the TLC's rise in minimum driver pay on 1 August 2025 raise what ride-hail drivers earn per mile and per minute with a passenger, and did base fares rise with it (Q6)?

## Method

- **Series.** Weekly, for Uber, Lyft and the two together (ride-hail), citywide, valid trips, each a ratio of sums over the week:
  - driver pay per mile: sum of driver pay ÷ sum of miles
  - driver pay per minute with a passenger: sum of driver pay ÷ sum of trip minutes
  - base fare per mile: sum of base fare ÷ sum of miles
- **Window.** The 12 ISO weeks before and the 12 after 2025-W31, the week containing 1 August 2025 (offset 0).
- **Transition week (D12).** 2025-W31 (28 Jul to 3 Aug) mixes days before and after the rise. It is drawn but left out of the before/after summary, and so is the same week of the reference year.
- **Seasonal reference.** The same ISO weeks of 2024, when pay rules didn't change on 1 August. They show the usual summer-to-autumn movement.
- **Before/after summary.** The median weekly value over the 12 weeks before and over the 12 weeks after; the change between them; and the change net of the 2024 reference, (1 + change in 2025) ÷ (1 + change in 2024) − 1. Medians keep one odd week from moving the summary.

## Findings

Short answer to Q6: around 1 August 2025 the data show no rise in what ride-hail drivers earned per mile or per minute with a passenger, and no rise in base fares. Pay did rise in 2025, but the step came earlier, around March.

1. **No rise shows across the week of 1 August 2025.** All figures are medians over the 12 weeks before and the 12 weeks after the transition week:
   - **Driver pay per mile:** $4.177 to $4.194 in 2025 (+0.4%). Over the same weeks of 2024 it rose more, $3.968 to $4.031 (+1.6%), so net of the seasonal pattern the change is **−1.2%**.
   - **Driver pay per minute with a passenger:** $1.049 to $1.043 (−0.5%), against +0.4% in 2024; net **−0.9%**.
   - **Uber and Lyft on their own:** both show the same thing. Net of 2024, Uber is −0.4% per mile and −1.4% per minute; Lyft is −1.9% per mile and −0.2% per minute.

   These net changes are about a point, smaller than the week-to-week movement in the weekly series (2025 pay per mile went from $4.305 in W26 to $3.929 in W27). So the estimated effect of the 1 August rule change is indistinguishable from zero. The data can't rule out a small rise hidden in that noise.
2. **Fares didn't rise with it.** Ride-hail base fare per mile went from $5.374 to $5.302 (−1.3%), against +0.9% over the same weeks of 2024: net **−2.2%**. Uber was −2.2% net, Lyft +1.7% net.
3. **2025 pay was already above 2024 before August.** In the 12 weeks before the transition week, ride-hail pay per mile was $4.177 against $3.968 a year earlier, and pay per minute $1.049 against $0.996. Pay per minute is above its 2024 value in every week of the window, and pay per mile in every week but W32. The gap is about the same size before and after 1 August. Whatever lifted 2025 pay above 2024 was in place before the window starts in May.
4. **The step came around March 2025.** The monthly pay per hour with a passenger (context table below):
   - **The step:** pay is $60.89 in February 2025, +2.2% on a year earlier, and $63.62 in March, +6.6% on a year earlier. That's +4.5% on the month before, against +0.2% from February to March 2024.
   - **After it:** from April to November 2025 pay stays between +3.7% and +6.1% above a year earlier.
   - **Across the August date:** July to August 2025 moves only +0.6% (July +4.2% on a year earlier, August +4.4%).
   - **2026:** there's a smaller step again from February to March 2026 (+2.4%).
5. **What the data can't say.** Trip records carry each trip's total driver pay, not the minimum-pay formula or the floor a trip was held to. Three readings fit the data, and this study can't choose between them:
   - The change in pay rates reached drivers around March 2025, not on the 1 August 2025 date in `config/periods.yaml`. That date needs checking against TLC's published rule.
   - The 1 August change raised a floor that most trips already cleared.
   - Platforms offset it elsewhere in pay.
6. **Trip mix doesn't explain the flat result.** An exploratory check on the same aggregates looked at the two years across the transition week. Miles per trip and minutes per trip rose by about the same small amount in both years, and average speed didn't change, so a shift between short and long trips doesn't hide a rise. This check isn't published as a table: it would need a new metric (see the lane's status log).

## Limits

- **Not a randomised test** (SPEC §12). The 2024 weeks take out the usual seasonal movement, but not anything else that changed between the two years and around August 2025, such as a platform's own pricing changes.
- **Per minute with a passenger.** Trip files have no time between trips (SPEC §12), so this is not pay per hour worked.
- **Driver pay excludes tips and tolls** in the trip records, and base fare excludes tolls, tips, taxes and fees (SPEC §12).
- **One date.** An event study at 1 August 2025 can't see a change that reached pay at another time; the monthly context table is there for that reason. Monthly values move by a point or two from month to month, so a step in them is clearest when it lasts, as the March 2025 one does.
- **Congestion pricing** was in force through both the before and after windows of 2025 and through neither window of 2024. It shifts the 2025 level against 2024 but not the change from before to after.
- **Each week counts once** in the medians, whatever its trip volume.
- **TLC may revise recent months** and does not guarantee vendor-submitted records (SPEC §12).
