## Question

Did the Congestion Relief Zone toll, which began on 5 January 2025, shift trips into or out of the zone below 60th Street, and which modes gained or lost (Q2)? Did ride-hail base fares or the driver's share of them move after it began (Q3)?

## Method

- **Design.** A year-over-year difference-in-differences by ISO week (SPEC §6). For each 2025 ISO week, against the same ISO week of 2024:
  - growth of a group = its 2025 value ÷ its 2024 value
  - estimated effect = growth of treated trips ÷ growth of control trips − 1

  Treated trips start or end in the congestion zone; control trips do neither. Comparing each group with itself a year earlier takes out the season. Comparing the two groups takes out whatever changed citywide between the years (weather, the economy, fleet size), as long as it changed both groups alike.
- **Measures,** each a ratio of sums over the week:
  - trips: every trip record, valid or not, as TLC counts them
  - base fare per mile: sum of base fare ÷ sum of miles, valid trips
  - driver share of base fare: sum of driver pay ÷ sum of base fare, valid ride-hail trips (Uber, Lyft, and the two together)
- **Modes.** Uber, Lyft, other ride-hail, yellow cabs and green cabs, plus ride-hail (the three ride-hail modes; Uber and Lyft only for driver share) and all modes.
- **Weeks.** From 2025-W02 (week of 6 Jan 2025). 2025-W01 has one day of pricing and is left out (D12). A week is also left out when a federal holiday falls in it in one year but not the other (SPEC §6; the list is in `config/periods.yaml`), or when the aggregates don't cover all seven of its days in both years. The sanity checks list every week left out and why.
- **Summary.** The median weekly estimated effect, with the 10th to 90th percentile range across weeks as its spread. Percentiles interpolate between weeks.
- **Zone lists (D9, D21).** The headline uses the core list, the zones wholly south of 60th Street. The wide list adds the borderline zones and is the sensitivity result. How the lists were drawn: [`reports/cbd_zones.md`](cbd_zones.md).

## Findings

Every figure here is an estimated effect: the median of 49 weekly estimates (2025-W02 to W52, less the two MLK Day weeks), with the 10th to 90th percentile range across those weeks in brackets. The core list is the headline; the wide list is the sensitivity check. None of this is a randomised test (see Limits).

1. **Trips touching the zone grew less than trips elsewhere.** For all modes together, the estimated effect is **−4.2%** (−7.4% to −0.9%). Trips touching the zone still rose a little in the median week (+1.7%), but trips elsewhere rose more (+6.8%; see "Growth of each group"). So this is a shortfall against the rest of the city, not an absolute fall.
2. **Ride-hail and yellow cabs both lost ground in the zone.** Ride-hail is **−8.1%** (−12.1% to −3.4%) and yellow cabs **−7.8%** (−12.6% to −3.8%). The two describe different paths:
   - Ride-hail trips touching the zone fell (−3.6% in the median week) while ride-hail elsewhere grew (+5.3%).
   - Yellow-cab trips touching the zone grew strongly (+16.7%), but yellow trips elsewhere grew faster still (+25.7%).

   The all-modes figure is smaller than either because cabs grew fastest across the city and are a much larger part of zone trips than of trips elsewhere (compare the cab and all-modes rows of the weekly trips tables). The mode mix differs between the groups, so the all-modes estimate isn't an average of the modes.
3. **Use the ride-hail total, not the companies one by one.** Uber is −9.8% (−14.5% to −2.2%) and Lyft −4.9% (−14.9% to +3.9%). Their weekly estimates drift in opposite directions through the year:
   - Uber goes from −16.6% in W02 to −6.3% in W50.
   - Lyft goes from +12.2% to −17.6% over the same weeks.

   Lyft grew much faster than Uber outside the zone (+16.1% against +1.6%). So the per-company estimates mix the toll with shifts between the two companies. Combined, ride-hail is steadier.
4. **Ride-hail base fares per mile rose on zone trips.** The estimated effect is **+3.0%** (+0.9% to +6.5%): Uber +4.2% (+1.9% to +7.9%), Lyft +1.9% (−1.9% to +5.3%). Base fare excludes the congestion fee itself, which riders pay on top. For yellow cabs, whose fares are set by a regulated meter rate, the estimate is +0.3% (−1.6% to +3.8%), close to zero as it should be if the design isn't inventing fare effects of its own.
5. **Drivers' share of the base fare fell on zone trips.** The estimated effect is **−3.7%** (−5.7% to −0.8%): Uber −4.2%, Lyft −3.0%. It fell by about as much as base fare per mile rose. So the extra base fare on zone trips went to the platforms' share of the fare, not to drivers (Q3).
6. **The wide list agrees, except for cabs.** With the borderline zones counted as inside, every ride-hail and all-modes estimate moves by 0.6 points or less. Yellow cabs move from −7.8% to −18.1%. That isn't because yellow trips touching the zone behave differently (+16.7% core, +15.1% wide). Under the wide list, yellow's control group loses the borderline Upper East and West Side and Central Park zones, and what's left grew much faster (+40.2% against +25.7%). The yellow estimate depends on what its comparison group contains, so neither yellow figure should be read on its own. Green cabs make few trips in the zone, and their estimates (+4.1%, range −5.1% to +15.6%) are too wide to read.
7. **Pickups by zone.** Of the 246 map zones with at least 100 pickups a day in 2024, 16 had fewer in 2025. The falls are small (none bigger than 3.4%), and seven of the ten largest are core-list zones. The ten largest rises (up to +26.5%) are all outside both zone lists, mostly in Queens and Brooklyn. This map has no control group, so it shows where pickups moved, not an estimated effect.

**Checks behind these findings.**
- Only the two MLK Day weeks are left out; every week from W02 to W52 is fully covered in both years.
- The weekly estimates stay within a steady band through the year. Two weeks stand out:
  - W37 (week of 8 Sep 2025), +4.9% for all modes, where zone trips jumped in 2025;
  - W52 (Christmas week), −10.4%.

  Medians aren't sensitive to single weeks.
- An exploratory check on the same aggregates found ride-hail trips touching the zone got slightly longer in 2025, not shorter, relative to trips elsewhere. Longer trips lower fare per mile, so a shift towards short trips doesn't explain the rise in finding 4. This check isn't published as a table: it would need a new metric (see the lane's status log).

## Limits

- **Not a randomised test** (SPEC §12). The estimate assumes that, without the toll, trips touching the zone and trips elsewhere would have grown alike from 2024 to 2025. Anything else that changed in the zone between the two years and not outside it (office attendance, events, transit service, street works) lands in the estimate.
- **Some control trips pay the toll.** A trip that drives through the zone without starting or ending there can carry the fee (SPEC §7.2 reports how many), yet counts as control. That pulls the estimate towards zero.
- **Riders can move across the line.** A rider who now gets out just north of 60th Street turns a treated trip into a control trip. That counts twice: once as a lost treated trip, once as a gained control trip.
- **Zones don't follow 60th Street.** Taxi zones straddle the boundary; the core and wide lists bracket that.
- **A mode's comparison group can move for its own reasons.** Where a mode's trips outside the zone grew very fast, as yellow cabs' did, or shifted between companies, as Uber's and Lyft's did, the estimate depends on what the comparison group holds (findings 3 and 6). The all-modes and ride-hail totals are the least exposed.
- **Cab record counts drift against TLC's own counts** in 2025 (D27). Drift spread evenly over a mode's trips cancels in the estimate, but it inflates the cab growth figures in "Growth of each group".
- **Base fare excludes tolls, tips, taxes and fees** (SPEC §12), so base fare per mile doesn't include the congestion fee itself. It shows whether platforms and meters moved the fare under the fee.
- **No surge flag and no rider identity** in trip files (SPEC §12), so pricing changes can't be split from demand changes.
- **Each week counts once** in the median and percentiles, whatever its trip volume.
- **TLC may revise recent months** and does not guarantee vendor-submitted records (SPEC §12).
